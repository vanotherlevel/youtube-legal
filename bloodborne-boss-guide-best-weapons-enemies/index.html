Bloodborne: How to Beat Every Boss, Best Weapons & Enemies

By vAnother Level · Updated 2026-11-12
Home › Guides › Bloodborne
On this page
  1. Every boss (in order)
  2. Best weapons
  3. Normal enemies

This guide covers every boss in Bloodborne and The Old Hunters DLC in the order you meet them, with each one's elemental weaknesses (fire, bolt, arcane, serrated, righteous), whether you can parry it or open it up for a visceral attack, and a concrete plan to beat it. After the bosses you get a tiered list of the best trick weapons and firearms with where to find them and how they scale, then a table of the normal enemies that kill the most hunters.

Five rules that make Bloodborne easier: 1) Stay aggressive after taking a hit: the Rally system gives back lost health if you hit back within a few seconds. 2) Your gun is a parry tool: shoot an enemy just as its attack winds up, then press R1 in front of it for a huge visceral attack. 3) Put your Blood Stone Shards into one weapon instead of spreading them around; upgrade level matters far more than which weapon you pick. 4) Carry Molotov Cocktails and Fire Paper for beasts, and Bolt Paper for Kin (cosmic and eldritch enemies). 5) Dodge into or past attacks, not away; most bosses punish backward rolls.
Weakness notes use the in-game defense values listed on the Bloodborne wikis (lower = weaker); advice assumes the final PS4 patch.

Every boss (in order)

Tick bosses off as you go. Story bosses you must beat to finish the game are Father Gascoigne, Vicar Amelia, Shadow of Yharnam, Rom, The One Reborn, Micolash, Mergo's Wet Nurse and Gehrman; everything else is optional, but most optional bosses guard great rewards. The order below is a common, smooth route; several optional bosses can be done earlier or later.

Cleric Beast

Where: the far end of the Great Bridge in Central Yharnam. Optional, but it drops the Sword Hunter Badge, which unlocks the Kirkhammer and the Repeating Pistol in the Messenger shop.

  • Weak to: fire and serrated damage (it is a beast). Resists: blunt, arcane and bolt.
  • Parry / visceral: don't rely on parries. Instead, shoot or hit its head until it collapses (roughly two pistol hits or three blunderbuss hits on NG), or break a limb by hitting the same leg or arm repeatedly. Both open a visceral attack.
  • What kills people: the huge left-arm grab and the forward leap. Roll toward it and past the grab, sprint sideways from the leap, and roll forward under the ground slam so you end up behind it.
  • Approach: live behind its back legs, hit a leg until it buckles, then go for the head. Molotovs from range add easy damage. When it heals its limbs with a red glow it gets angrier but does not regain health. Summoning Father Gascoigne (or Alfred later) pulls its attention off you.

Father Gascoigne

Where: Tomb of Oedon at the end of Central Yharnam. Required. Drops the Oedon Tomb Key.

  • Weak to: fire in both forms (even more in beast form). Serrated damage only gets its bonus in the final beast phase.
  • Parry / visceral: yes, he is the game's parry tutorial. Shoot him as his axe or cleaver swing starts, then visceral. Viscerals work in all phases.
  • Tiny Music Box: get it from the little girl in the window near the first lamp (talk to her and take the job). Using it in his human phases makes him clutch his head, giving you a free charged attack and visceral.
  • What kills people: the long axe-form sweeps and, in phase 3, the fast multi-swipe beast flurry. Fight among the gravestones in phase 1 and 2: he often clips them, leaving him open.
  • Phase 3 (beast): he has hyper armor and destroys tombstones. Keep mid-range, dodge sideways through the flurry, and shoot him mid-flurry for a visceral. Molotovs and the Oil Urns found earlier in the area hit hard here.

Blood-Starved Beast

Where: Church of the Good Chalice at the bottom of Old Yharnam. Optional, but it gives the Pthumeru Chalice (Chalice Dungeons) and is the most common early wall.

  • Weak to: fire and serrated. Resists: arcane and bolt. It is highly resistant to poison but builds Slow Poison on you, so bring Antidotes (three more sit on the altar in the arena).
  • Parry / visceral: yes, in every phase. It is one of the easiest bosses to parry; six or seven viscerals can kill it.
  • What kills people: the long, fast swipe chains and the poison explosion in later phases. Stepping to its right side (your left) dodges most of its swings, since they sweep forward and to its left.
  • Approach: the Flamesprayer (bought from the Messengers) is the classic cheese: it costs no stamina and the beast hates fire. Otherwise, parry the single swipes or the last hit of a chain. A Pungent Blood Cocktail distracts it for a free hit. At 66% and 33% it roars and gets faster.
  • Help: Alfred can be summoned with the Old Hunter Bell if you meet him before Vicar Amelia.

Vicar Amelia

Where: inside the Grand Cathedral at the top of Cathedral Ward. Required.

  • Weak to: fire (very) and serrated. Resists: bolt, arcane and blunt. Fire Paper on a Saw Cleaver or Saw Spear is ideal.
  • Parry / visceral: not a parry boss. Instead, damage her legs until she drops to her knees, or hit her head; when her head is down you can visceral her.
  • Healing: below half health she can heal herself. Numbing Mist blocks the heal for about 30 seconds; a fast attack or a gun shot during the prayer animation also interrupts it.
  • What kills people: her leaping slam and long claw combos. Stay close, dodge forward and to the side under her arms, and stay near her legs rather than her face.
  • Help: summons are locked until you open the Cathedral Ward gate (the Hunter Chief Emblem is the fastest way). Old Hunter Henriett makes this fight much easier because Amelia loves to chase her.

Witch of Hemwick

Where: the end of Hemwick Charnel Lane. Optional. You get the Rune Workshop Tool from a cellar that opens after the fight.

  • Weak to: nothing special; they actually resist bolt a bit. Plain physical damage and backstabs are best.
  • Parry / visceral: the witches are very easy to backstab, and a backstab leads straight into a visceral.
  • The trick: there are two witches who turn invisible and summon Mad Ones. Ignore the Mad Ones and look for the faint red glow or debris moving as a witch walks. Hit her with a charged attack, then keep swinging.
  • Phase 2: each witch can revive the other. Bring the second witch down to low health first, then finish both close together.
  • Insight: Mad Ones do not spawn if you are at 0 Insight, which is an easy way to make a retry trivial.

Shadow of Yharnam

Where: the very bottom of the Forbidden Woods. Required. Three hooded hunters: a sword Shadow, a candle Shadow and a mage.

  • Weak to: bolt (Bolt Paper or Tonitrus). Fire is poor here. Per the wiki tables, the mage-type Shadow resists bolt and arcane more than the others.
  • Parry / visceral: yes, all three can be parried and visceraled.
  • Phase 1: kill or cripple the mage first if you can; its homing fireballs stack up fast. Use the big gravestones and trees to block fireballs and to split the melee Shadows away.
  • Phase 2 (one drops below about 40%): they sprout long reaching limbs and the mage switches to arcing fireballs that leave fire on the ground. Hit the second Shadow while it lights its sword to stop the fire buff.
  • Phase 3 (two left low or one dead): the last one kneels and summons huge snakes that can one-shot you. Keep hitting it during the summon to cancel it.
  • Tip: get all three near 30% before any transforms; they do not heal between phases. An NPC summon draws the melee Shadows off you.

Darkbeast Paarl

Where: Hypogean Gaol. Get there by letting a Snatcher kill you in Cathedral Ward, or from Yahar'gul; drop down from the prison area to its arena. Optional. Killing it opens a shortcut to Old Yharnam and drops the Spark Hunter Badge (unlocks Tonitrus).

  • Weak to: fire and arcane, plus serrated (it is a beast). Resists: bolt heavily and blunt somewhat. Henryk's set helps against its electricity.
  • Parry / visceral: no parry. Knock it down by hitting its legs and head (each leg has hidden health), then visceral its head for about a third of its health.
  • What kills people: the lightning Nova (its chest glows first) and electric breath (head glows). Stand behind its tail for the Nova, and roll under its belly during swipes.
  • Break the charge: oil plus Molotovs, or enough hits to its skull, will knock out its electric field for a few seconds. Chase it when it backs off to recharge, so it can't.
  • Help: Defector Antal can be summoned. In NG+ it recharges much faster.

Rom, the Vacuous Spider

Where: Moonside Lake. Jump into the lake from the Lunarium balcony at Byrgenwerth. Required; killing Rom starts the Blood Moon.

  • Weak to: bolt (very), then fire and arcane. She is Kin. Her head has high physical defense, so hit her body instead.
  • Parry / visceral: none to speak of. This fight is about clearing adds and burning her down.
  • Phase 1: she sits still and spawns small spiders. Kill the spiders first, since they stunlock you, then hit Rom freely. Each time she teleports, a new pack spawns.
  • Phase 2 and 3: she adds arcane ice that falls from the sky and erupts from the ground, plus an explosion around herself. Keep moving in a wide circle and dodge in alternating directions.
  • Tip: Bolt Paper on a heavy weapon is the best damage plan. A charged heavy hit can interrupt her teleport, which means fewer spider waves.

The One Reborn

Where: Advent Plaza in Yahar'gul, Unseen Village, under the Blood Moon. Required; it blocks the way to the Nightmare of Mensis.

  • Weak to: bolt (very) and fire. Resists: arcane heavily.
  • Parry / visceral: no parry. Its big top corpse takes extra damage when it collapses.
  • Kill the Bell Ringers first: run past the boss, climb the stairs in the corners and kill the casters on the upper walkways. They power its spells.
  • What kills people: the rain of flesh (keep moving or backstep repeatedly), the vomit flood (back off immediately), and being trampled at low health. Stay between its middle legs, or break one big leg for bonus damage.
  • Approach: with the Ringers gone it is slow and easy to stagger. Bolt Paper and charged attacks on the legs end it quickly.

Martyr Logarius

Where: the rooftop of Forsaken Cainhurst Castle (reach Cainhurst with the Summons to Vileblood from Alfred). Optional. Drops the Crown of Illusions. Light the Cainhurst lamp first.

  • Weak to: fire and bolt; he resists arcane (his spells are arcane). He is immune to poison.
  • Parry / visceral: parry is possible but tricky. Easier: when he plants his sword to buff himself (at roughly the middle of the fight), get behind him and backstab for a visceral. Gun shots stagger him well in his melee phase.
  • Phase 1: he throws tracking skulls and orbs. Use the rooftop pillars to break line of sight, hit two or three times and dodge left of the exploding skull.
  • The buff: while his aura is up, bullets bounce off. A backstab or a well-timed shot breaks it, and his defense drops while he channels, so go all in.
  • Phase 2 and 3: he chases you with sword and staff combos and starts leaping. Bait his long stab, roll past, and punish. Dodge his landing slams and hit him while he is stunned.

Celestial Emissary

Where: Lumenflower Gardens in Upper Cathedral Ward. Open the Ward with the Upper Cathedral Key, found in Yahar'gul (Yahar'gul Chapel area), at the locked door at the top of the Healing Church Workshop. Optional.

  • Weak to: bolt, then fire.
  • Find the real one: dozens of small blue Celestials attack you. The real Emissary is the one without a health bar over its head.
  • Visceral: get behind it during its growth transformation for a charged attack and visceral, which can end the fight early.
  • Phases: below half it grows huge; around a quarter it sprouts tentacles and fires a barrage of six cosmic lasers. Run in a circle or use the central pillar, or stay close so it doesn't use the lasers.
  • Tip: wide sweeping weapons clear the small Celestials. They all die when the real one dies.

Ebrietas, Daughter of the Cosmos

Where: Altar of Despair. After the Celestial Emissary, break the window in its arena and take the lift down. Optional, but a favourite fight.

  • Weak to: bolt and fire, and especially thrust damage. She is Kin. Her head takes bonus damage.
  • Parry / visceral: no parry. Hit her head (a Cannon shot or a heavy charged hit works) until she drops it, then visceral.
  • What kills people: the forward charge. At long range dodge straight out of its path; up close, standing at her chest is safer than running. Her laser is avoided by running in a wide circle.
  • Positioning: stand between her tentacles at her side, unlocked, and thrust. Keep open space behind you so you are never pinned to a wall.
  • Phase 2 (about half health): she releases a damaging aura and uses A Call Beyond. Back off when the aura starts, heal, then return. A heavy hit can interrupt the aura.
  • Best plan: Rakuyo or another thrust weapon with Bolt Paper, standing between her tentacles.

Amygdala

Where: Nightmare Frontier. Get the Tonsil Stone from one of the house NPCs after nightfall, then let the Lesser Amygdala at the side of the Grand Cathedral square grab you. Optional; drops the Ailing Loran Chalice.

  • Weak to: arcane, fire and bolt roughly equally; very vulnerable to the A Call Beyond spell. Resists blunt.
  • Parry / visceral: no parry. Damage its head (guns and Executioner's Gloves reach it) to drop it for a visceral; there are two chances, the second near the end.
  • What kills people: standing still. Keep moving, and dodge its jump slam rather than waiting under it. Hit the same arm repeatedly; a bleeding arm takes more damage.
  • Phase 3 (around 30%): it rips off two arms to use as clubs and gets much more dangerous. Stay behind it and hit its legs and tail.

Micolash, Host of the Nightmare

Where: Nightmare of Mensis, reached through Yahar'gul after the One Reborn falls. Required.

  • Weak to: no strong elemental weakness, and he resists arcane. Poison works well (Poison Knives), as do firearms boosted with Bone Marrow Ash.
  • Phase 1: he runs away through the maze. Chase him into the dead-end room; his only attack here is Augur of Ebrietas, which you can sidestep and punish with a backstab.
  • Phase 2 (after 50%): he teleports and gets aggressive. Stay glued to him so he only punches.
  • What kills people: A Call Beyond, a barrage of arcane stars that can one-shot you from full health. Shoot him early in the cast to interrupt it, or dodge toward him as it fires.

Mergo's Wet Nurse

Where: Mergo's Loft at the top of the Nightmare of Mensis. Required. Drops a One Third of Umbilical Cord.

  • Weak to: bolt, fire and arcane roughly equally, and very vulnerable to Rapid Poison (Poison Knives).
  • Parry / visceral: no parry. Win by staying behind her.
  • What kills people: the blade flurry. Don't run away: get to her back, where few of the blades reach.
  • Purple fog: she blankets the arena in darkness and a clone copies her attacks. Lock onto the real one and circle; one hit kills the clone. Hitting her while she casts can stop the fog.
  • Tip: most of her swings can be rolled under. Watch which way the arms go and roll to her left side.

Gehrman, the First Hunter

Where: the Hunter's Dream, after Mergo's Wet Nurse. Talk to him and choose to refuse his offer (or attack him). Required for two of the three endings.

  • Weak to: arcane, bolt and fire all slightly; he is immune to poison. Bolt or Fire Paper and Tonitrus all work.
  • Parry / visceral: yes, for the whole fight. Be careful: his own gun can parry you, so don't swing into his raised pistol.
  • Phase 1: scythe charges, leaps and wind gusts. Bait an attack, step back, hit once.
  • Phase 2 (about a third down): curved sword and blunderbuss; he gets in your face with long combos. Stay close, dash around him and punish when a combo ends.
  • Midpoint channel: he stops and charges for a few seconds in the open. Run behind him for a charged attack and visceral. Under half health he can unleash a big energy blast; get out of range.
  • Moon Presence: if you used three One Third of Umbilical Cord before beating him, the Moon Presence fight follows. Drops the Old Hunter Badge (Burial Blade in the shop).

Moon Presence

Where: the Hunter's Dream right after Gehrman, only if you consumed three Umbilical Cords. Optional; it leads to the third ending.

  • Weak to: bolt and fire, like most late bosses. Bolt Paper, Fire Paper or Tonitrus are all good.
  • Visceral: it can be visceraled; a charged attack while it winds up its big cone attack can stagger it.
  • Gaze (the red cone): can't be dodged and drops you to 1 HP, but cannot kill you on its own and leaves it worn out for a long time. Attack right away to Rally health back, then heal. Don't let it land while you are poisoned or under its blood effects.
  • Healing block: its blood rain and the black fluid behind its tail stop you using Blood Vials for a while. Avoid the area behind it after its combos.
  • Approach: stay mid-range, bait the three-hit combo, dodge and punish with one or two hits. Sidestep the jump smash and the grab.

The Old Hunters DLC bosses

How to enter: after you beat Vicar Amelia, a Messenger in the Hunter's Dream gives you the Eye of a Blood-drunk Hunter. Go to the courtyard side of Oedon Chapel in Cathedral Ward and let the Lesser Amygdala grab you; it carries you to the Hunter's Nightmare. The DLC is tough, so most players go in after Rom. Light the first lamp straight away.

Ludwig, the Accursed / Holy Blade (DLC)

Where: the Nightmare Grand Cathedral in the Hunter's Nightmare. Required for the DLC. Afterwards, wear any Church attire and talk to his head (or kill it) in the Underground Corpse Pile to get the Holy Moonlight Sword; this is missable if you kill Laurence first.

  • Weak to: fire in both phases. Arcane is good in phase 1 but he resists it in phase 2; bolt is decent. Whether he takes the serrated bonus is disputed between wiki pages, so don't build around it.
  • Parry / visceral: not a parry fight. In phase 1, repeated hits to his head stagger him for a visceral to the face. In phase 2, enough damage staggers him for a visceral from the front.
  • Phase 1 (Accursed): a horse-beast that charges around the arena. Stay under him or beside him, not directly behind (back kicks). Each side of his body can be injured and takes more damage, and he does not heal it. At 75% he adds an arcane vomit spray and a screaming charge.
  • Phase 2 (Holy Blade, 50%): an entirely new moveset with the glowing sword. Close in instead of waiting and dodge through his slashes. When he points his sword up, back off: a big explosion and shockwaves follow.
  • Help: Valtr, Henriett and the Madaras Twin can be summoned.

Laurence, the First Vicar (DLC)

Where: the Grand Cathedral in the Hunter's Nightmare. The only optional DLC boss: pick up Laurence's Skull, then return to his body on the altar. Drops Beast's Embrace.

  • Weak to: physical damage (serrated gets its bonus), then arcane and bolt by a small margin. He massively resists fire, so leave the Molotovs at home and wear fire defense (Charred Hunter set).
  • Parry / visceral: break his head for a visceral; pistols, the Piercing Rifle or a heavy hit after his grab or triple pound do it. His head regenerates, so you can repeat the stagger.
  • Phase 1 (to about 70%): a fiery Cleric Beast. Stay at his side or back and dodge toward his right when he swings.
  • Phase 2 (70-40%): more aggressive, with lava rising from the floor. Don't get cornered.
  • Phase 3 (below 40%): his legs break and he crawls, leaving lava trails and spitting fire. Save bullets and vials for this part; attack mostly after his weak-arm swipes and dodge behind him when he spits.
  • Help: Valtr can be summoned. A Loch Shield absorbs much of the area fire damage.

Living Failures (DLC)

Where: Lumenwood Garden, reached by rotating the stairs in the Research Hall. Drops the Astral Clocktower Key.

  • Weak to: bolt (they are Kin) and Rapid Poison. Resists: arcane. Tonitrus and Bolt Paper are ideal.
  • Shared health: up to four Failures all share one health bar. Focus whichever one is most isolated.
  • Visceral: you can backstab and visceral them during spawns, casting and the meteor storm; a visceral gives brief invincibility.
  • Phase 2 (about 60%): they all raise their hands and call a meteor storm. Run counter-clockwise or hide behind the big tree; meteors come from a fixed direction.
  • Poison trick: Rapid Poison drains the shared pool, and a strong poison build can finish them before the meteors if you don't kill an individual Failure first.

Lady Maria of the Astral Clocktower (DLC)

Where: inside the Astral Clocktower, after the Living Failures. Required for the DLC; drops the Celestial Dial and unlocks her set in the shop.

  • Weak to: arcane and bolt slightly. She resists fire strongly, especially once her blades ignite.
  • Parry / visceral: yes, in all three phases. Parry-heavy play is the fastest route; three or four viscerals can skip straight past phase 2.
  • Phase 1: Rakuyo combos and the occasional Evelyn shot. Dodge to your left for most attacks; hit two to four times, then fire your gun to catch her mid-swing.
  • Phase 2: she stabs herself, her blades trail blood and reach further, and she gains an attack that can't be parried. Learn the longer reach before committing.
  • Phase 3: she floats, draws in blood and bursts it out, then her swings leave fire in their wake. Even after a parry the fire can still hit you, so be careful about going for the visceral.

Orphan of Kos (DLC)

Where: the shore at the end of the Fishing Hamlet. The final DLC boss and one of the hardest in the game. Drops the Kos Parasite.

  • Weak to: no real elemental weakness; physical and blood damage are best. Immune to poison.
  • Parry / visceral: most of his melee can be parried in both phases. In phase 1 his slow overhead smash is the easiest (shoot as he lifts his leg). Dodging under his forward leap sets up a backstab.
  • Phase 1: constant attacks with mixed speeds, leaps and thrown blood. Stay on his left side (your right) and circle; fast weapons work best. Dodge sideways from projectiles.
  • Phase 2 (around 50%): a shockwave knocks you down (no damage), then he becomes bigger and faster and calls lightning with a scream. Break lock-on, sprint between the lightning waves, then re-engage. Parries are riskier now; the safest is when he walks at you with the weapon held low.
  • Tip: fight in the shallow water, which has fewer obstacles than the beach. He is briefly open after his shockwave and lightning screams: use those moments to heal or buff.

Normal enemies

Normal enemies kill more first-time hunters than bosses do, usually in groups. Pull enemies one at a time, look around corners, and treat any humanoid as a parry target.

Frequently asked questions

What is the hardest boss in Bloodborne?

Most players name the Orphan of Kos from The Old Hunters DLC. In the base game, Martyr Logarius, Ebrietas and the Blood-Starved Beast (early in the game) stop the most people.

What is the best weapon in Bloodborne?

There is no single best weapon, but Ludwig's Holy Blade, the Holy Moonlight Sword, the Saw Cleaver, the Kirkhammer, the Chikage and the Blades of Mercy are top picks. Match the weapon to your stats and put all your upgrades into it.

Which Bloodborne bosses are optional?

Cleric Beast, Blood-Starved Beast, Witch of Hemwick, Darkbeast Paarl, Martyr Logarius, Celestial Emissary, Ebrietas, Amygdala and Moon Presence are optional, as is Laurence in the DLC. The story bosses are Gascoigne, Vicar Amelia, Shadow of Yharnam, Rom, The One Reborn, Micolash, Mergo's Wet Nurse and Gehrman.

How do I fight the Moon Presence?

Consume all three One Third of Umbilical Cord items before you beat Gehrman, then refuse his offer and defeat him. The Moon Presence fight starts right after the cutscene.

Which bosses can you parry in Bloodborne?

The human-sized bosses and some beasts: Father Gascoigne, the Blood-Starved Beast, the Shadows of Yharnam, Gehrman, Lady Maria and the Orphan of Kos are all reliably parryable, and Martyr Logarius can be parried with difficult timing. Large monsters like Rom, Amygdala and Ebrietas cannot; you stagger them by hitting weak spots instead.

Is serrated or righteous damage better?

Serrated is more useful because it boosts damage against beasts, which make up most of Yharnam and many bosses. Righteous only helps against a small group of enemies, mostly in Cainhurst Castle.

When should I start The Old Hunters DLC?

You can enter after beating Vicar Amelia, but the Hunter's Nightmare is tuned for late-game characters. Most players go in after Rom or near the end of the base game.
