GTA 6 Easy Mode & Accessibility: Every Setting Explained

Yes, GTA 6 has a big accessibility menu: menu narration (a screen reader), audio descriptions for cutscenes, High Contrast Shaders, colour-blind modes, an accessible combat option that lowers enemy health and the damage you take, QTE and button-hold assists, and Auto Drive. What it doesn't have is a way for a fully blind player to get around the open world alone, and early reports disagree on whether you can fully remap buttons.

Rockstar didn't send review copies to accessibility outlets, so most of what's below comes from the official PlayStation Store listing, launch-day reviews and accessibility writers who pieced it together from streams. Every claim is labelled so you know which is which.

OFFICIAL: Rockstar's PlayStation Store listing for GTA VI tags 30 accessibility features in 5 groups

Every accessibility feature on the official store listing

Sony's store pages now carry an accessibility section filled in by the publisher. The GTA VI listing names these features:

"Basic" and "Advanced" are Sony's own labels for how deep an option goes. "Screen Reader (Basic)" means menus are read out, not the whole game world.

Vision: narration, audio description and high contrast

REPORTED: Can I Play That (Marijn Rongen, Nov 18-19), Double Tap (Nov 18), Eurogamer review via Double Tap, Kotaku live blog (Nov 19, 12:02 PM ET)
  • Menu narration (screen reader): reads most menus and the UI. Some streams show the game asking players to turn it on at first launch, so you can set it up without sighted help.
  • Audio descriptions: a separate toggle that describes what happens in cutscenes only. It does not describe the open world.
  • High Contrast Shaders: highlights characters, enemies and things you can interact with against the background. Accessibility writer Laura Kate Dale noted on launch day (via Kotaku) that the colours can't be customised.
  • Colour-blind settings: boost the colours of focus trails.
  • Size options: UI text size, subtitle size, objective marker size and map icon size, plus UI colours and reticle look.
  • Minimap and compass: the minimap can be set to always on, and a compass can sit at the top of the screen.

Can blind players play GTA 6?

Not on their own yet, according to Double Tap, the blind-gaming show. Narration covers menus and audio description covers cutscenes, but there's no audio guidance to a destination and nothing that describes the street around you. Blind reviewer Victor Dima told the outlet he wouldn't call it fully accessible for blind players. Double Tap still called it a strong set for players with low vision.

THEORY: Rockstar could add navigation help in a patch, as some studios have, but Rockstar hasn't said anything about it

Hearing: subtitles, captions and tinnitus reduction

  • Subtitles and captions for non-speech and environmental sounds, both resizable, with an optional background box and speaker names.
  • Tinnitus reduction: turns off the high-pitched ringing after explosions.
  • Mono audio for players with hearing in one ear.
  • Separate volume sliders for sound effects, UI, dialogue, music and media (radio and TV).
  • Reviewable recent dialogue, so you can re-read what a character just said.

Motor: holds, QTEs, controls and Auto Drive

  • Tap assist for quick-time events: five levels, per Can I Play That.
  • Hold assists: turn button holds into toggles, and set a hold delay to stop accidental presses.
  • Simplified minigames.
  • Three controller presets, including a left-handed layout (Eurogamer via Double Tap). Stick and trigger sensitivity and haptics are adjustable.
  • Auto Drive: the car drives itself to your waypoint.
  • Aim assist levels, plus drive-by and shooting assists. Sprint sits on L3 by default, and you can change it.
Remapping is the one open question. The store listing says "Controller Remapping (Basic)", but Can I Play That couldn't confirm full remapping from footage and believes it isn't there. What is confirmed: three presets, and sprint can be moved off L3. Check Settings > Controller in your copy.

Does GTA 6 have an easy mode? Two toggles work like one

No, GTA 6 has no Easy, Normal or Hard mode, but two settings do the same job: Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage. Both start switched off. Turn on both and gunfights and chases get much more forgiving, and you keep your trophies.

REPORTED: Push Square (Robert Ramsey, Nov 19), Notebookcheck via Eurogamer (Daniel Zlatev, Nov 19, updated Nov 20), GGRecon (Jake Bannister, Nov 20)
SettingWhat it doesTrophies
Accessible CombatLowers enemy health and cuts the damage enemies deal to you. Notebookcheck says it works like a difficulty selector.Stay on (Push Square tested it)
Reduced Vehicle DamageYour car, bike or boat takes less damage from gunfire, crashes and hazards (Notebookcheck).Stay on (Push Square tested it)
  1. Open Settings, then the Gameplay tab (Notebookcheck names this tab).
  2. Find Accessible Combat and switch it on.
  3. Switch on Reduced Vehicle Damage in the same place.
  4. Confirm the warning. The game says the change will significantly alter the balance and experience. Every outlet reports this warning, each in slightly different words.
Neither toggle makes you invincible. Push Square says so plainly, and Notebookcheck calls them "far from a true invincibility mode". You can still die on hard missions, so keep healing and armour up (see how to heal in GTA 6).

Easy mode vs cheats: which one to use

The toggles are the safe choice. Notebookcheck says they don't lock progress, stop saving or restrict achievements. Cheats are the opposite. They only unlock after the main story, and once you use one you can't save (PC Gamer, Notebookcheck). So if a mission is too hard, use the two toggles, not a cheat.

Other help in the same menus: mission objectives can be reviewed at any time, help text and reminders can stay on, minigames can be simplified, and there's an Auto Drive option. That fits the store's "Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)" and "Game Speed (Basic)" tags.

REPORTED: Notebookcheck counts 25 gameplay, 12 display, 70 interface, 24 camera and 14 audio accessibility options. No other outlet has published a count yet

Comfort: motion sickness and gore

  • Motion blur, head bob and screen-effect intensity (hits and player status) can be lowered or turned off.
  • Several camera movement settings, a lower flashbang intensity and an overall brightness reduction toggle.
  • Blood and gore can be reduced, and dismemberment can be turned off.

For the rest of the menu (aim assist curves, camera, HUD), see our GTA 6 best settings guide. If your screenshots look faded, that's the HDR capture fix, not an accessibility setting.

Where to find the settings

Accessibility options aren't in one tab. Reports place them across the UI, Display, Camera, Audio and Controller tabs, with a group inside Gameplay. High Contrast Shaders are in Display. Your console's own narrator (PS5 or Xbox) is separate from the game's menu narration, so turn off one of them if both talk at once.

Frequently asked questions

Does GTA 6 have a screen reader?

Yes. Menu narration reads most menus and the UI, and the store lists it as Screen Reader (Basic). A separate toggle adds audio descriptions to cutscenes.

Does turning on Accessible Combat disable trophies?

No. Push Square tested it: Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage don't disable trophies. Cheats are different, because you can't save after using one.

Can blind players play GTA 6?

Not independently yet, per Double Tap. Menus and cutscenes are covered, but there's no audio navigation or description of the open world.

Does GTA 6 have an easy mode?

Not as a difficulty level. Two settings in the Gameplay tab work like one: Accessible Combat (weaker enemies, less damage to you) and Reduced Vehicle Damage. Both start off.

Can you remap buttons in GTA 6?

Partly. The store lists Controller Remapping (Basic) and there are three presets including left-handed, but full free remapping hasn't been confirmed.
