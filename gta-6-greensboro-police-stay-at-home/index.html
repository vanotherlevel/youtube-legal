Update, November 22: when GTA 6 launched on November 19, police forces in the US, Hong Kong and the UK joined in. Most of them made the same point with a joke: enjoy Leonida, but real crimes have real penalties and there's no reload. Here's what each department said, with the original reports linked below.

Real posts from official police accounts, as reported by FOX 5 Atlanta, WKRC/WSET, LEVEL UP and Digit (Nov 19, 2026)

Launch week: what each police force said

Aurora's post had the best line for GTA fans. In the game, Leonida's cops hunt you on a wanted level you can shake by hiding or switching cars. Aurora said that doesn't work there: "One star is plenty and will lead to you in handcuffs." If you want to know how the wanted system really works in GTA 6, read our wanted level and police guide.

CONTEXT: none of these posts says the game causes crime. FOX 5 Atlanta notes the department made no such claim. They're public-safety messages riding the biggest launch of the year.

Guernsey's warning to parents

Guernsey's message was the serious one. Laura Simpson, digital safety lead for Guernsey Police, warned parents that the game has a lot of blood and gore, plus decapitations and unhealthy relationships. She added that adults' memories of older GTA games don't tell them how their kids will feel playing this one. GTA 6 is rated 18+, but Digit notes children can still see it on streams or by watching their parents play.

Before launch: Greensboro's viral post

Even the police are counting down to GTA 6. The Greensboro Police Department in North Carolina posted on X that Rockstar's game needs to "hurry up and get here" so people stay home and officers can "catch a break". The post passed 1 million views, and Dexerto picked it up today.

Real post from the official @GSO_Police account (November 6, 2026)

The post

GTA 6 needs to hurry up and get here so y'all can stay at home and let us catch a break.

— Greensboro Police Department (@GSO_Police) November 6, 2026

When we checked it on November 9, it had about 39,000 likes, 3,100 reposts and 300 replies. It's clearly a joke, and that's how the replies took it. Nobody is claiming a video game release changes crime numbers.

Then vs now: from Hot Coffee to 'please release it'

Here's why this one is funny if you've followed the series for a while. Twenty years ago, Grand Theft Auto was the game politicians and lawyers lined up to attack:

  • 2005: the "Hot Coffee" scandal hit GTA: San Andreas. Then-Senator Hillary Clinton called for a Federal Trade Commission investigation into the game.
  • 2000s: attorney Jack Thompson ran years of lawsuits and campaigns against GTA. He was permanently disbarred in Florida in 2008, the same year GTA IV came out.
  • 2026: a US police department is publicly asking for GTA 6 to come out sooner.

The police are in the game too

The joke works both ways. Rockstar's trailers are full of Leonida's version of the news: police body-cam clips, a corrections mugshot and a TV segment titled "Dirt-Bike Dirt-Bags". Our hero shot is an officer from Trailer 2.

GTA 6 is out now

GTA 6 came out on November 19, 2026, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. For your first hours, see Every Gang Explained, our money guide and the GTA 6 news hub.

Frequently asked questions

Did police warn GTA 6 players?

Yes. At the November 19 launch, police in Atlanta, South Fulton, Aurora (Colorado), Hong Kong, Derbyshire and Guernsey posted messages. Most were jokes reminding players that real crimes have real penalties.

What did Aurora police say about GTA 6?

Aurora told 'Vice City transplants' that stealing a car can still be a felony and that it has no six-star system: 'One star is plenty and will lead to you in handcuffs.'

Did police say GTA 6 causes crime?

No. The posts are public-safety messages. FOX 5 Atlanta notes the Atlanta post doesn't claim playing GTA leads to real crime.

Did Greensboro police really say they want GTA 6 out?

Yes, as a joke. On November 6, 2026, @GSO_Police posted that GTA 6 needs to hurry up so people stay home and officers catch a break.
