GTA 6 Hacker Arion Kurtaj Gets 7 Years, Freed on Licence Same Day

Arion Kurtaj, the hacker behind the huge 2022 GTA 6 leak, has reportedly been sentenced to seven years and released on licence the same day (November 23, 2026). He had pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court on November 16, three days before the game launched.

REPORTED: per the Crown Prosecution Service and BBC, via news outlets

The sentence: seven years, released the same day

Arion Kurtaj was reportedly sentenced to seven years at Woolwich Crown Court on Monday, November 23, 2026 and released on licence the same day, four days after GTA 6 launched. So he was not cleared and did not walk away without a sentence: the time he had already spent held counted against it.

REPORTED: court reports via Yahoo News, TweakTown, Inquisitr and SixShore. We have not seen an official sentencing transcript.

How a seven-year sentence meant walking out

  • The starting term: Mr Justice Turner reportedly set a nine-year term.
  • The reduction: he cut it by two years for the time Kurtaj spent under a hospital order, leaving seven.
  • Time already held: with that time counted, Kurtaj was eligible for immediate release.
  • On licence: this is the UK system where the rest of a sentence is served in the community under conditions.

Reported conditions

Court reports say a five-year Serious Crime Prevention Order restricts his online activity, including a ban on using Telegram and Discord. The police statement reportedly does not confirm that the order has already been made, so treat that part with care. The full licence terms have not been published.

The judge told him "You are a very talented young man", followed by a warning: "Don't let this happen again."

What he pleaded guilty to

According to reports citing the UK's Crown Prosecution Service (CPS), Kurtaj, now 21 and formerly of Oxfordshire, admitted six offences linked to his time in the Lapsus$ hacking group:

  • Three unauthorised acts to impair the operation of a computer (Computer Misuse Act 1990).
  • One count of fraud (Fraud Act 2006).
  • One count of securing unauthorised access to computer material with intent.
  • One count of possessing a mobile phone in prison.

Reports say the admitted offences cover attacks on Rockstar Games, Uber, BT/EE and Revolut, most of them committed when he was 16 and 17.

The Rockstar hack in 2022, in short

  • September 2022: 90 clips from an early, unfinished build of GTA 6 were posted online under the name "TeaPotUberHacker". The BBC says they showed close to an hour of gameplay.
  • The threat: in a Slack message to Rockstar staff, he threatened to start releasing the game's source code unless Rockstar contacted him within 24 hours. The prosecution account, as reported, does not say any code was released.
  • The cost: the BBC reports Rockstar said recovering from the hack cost $5 million and thousands of staff hours.
  • The Fire Stick: he was on bail in a Travelodge in Bicester when he did it, with his computer taken away. He got online with an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into the hotel TV, plus a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse.
Rockstar confirmed the leak was real in September 2022 and said it would not delay development. The game released on November 19 was four years further along than those clips. See how far it came in every new GTA 6 mechanic, all 274.

Why there is a second case at all

This is not the first time Kurtaj has been in court over GTA 6. Here's the timeline as reported:

DateWhat happened
Aug 2023A jury at Southwark Crown Court found he committed the acts in all 12 charges. He had been ruled unfit for a normal trial because of his autism, so it was a "trial of the facts" (did he do it), not a guilty verdict.
Dec 2023He was given an indefinite hospital order: a secure hospital unless doctors judged him no longer a danger.
Late 2025A routine assessment found him fit to stand a criminal trial (BBC, via reports).
Jan 2026Moved from hospital to a normal prison (BBC, via reports).
Nov 16, 2026Pleaded guilty to six offences at Woolwich Crown Court (CPS, via reports).
Nov 23, 2026Sentenced to seven years at Woolwich Crown Court and released on licence the same day (reported).

CPS Specialist Prosecutor Robert Hutchinson is quoted as saying Kurtaj "used sophisticated hacking techniques to carry out a sustained campaign of cyberattacks".
