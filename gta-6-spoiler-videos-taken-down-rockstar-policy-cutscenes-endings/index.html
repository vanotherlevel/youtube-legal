GTA 6 Spoiler Videos Can Be Taken Down: Rockstar's Rules Explained

By vAnother Level · Updated 2026-11-18

Yes, GTA 6 spoiler videos can be taken down, even after the game launches on November 19. Rockstar's policy on posting its copyrighted material lists videos showing the ending, edited cutscene compilations and isolated cinematics as things Take-Two can remove, while cutscenes shown inside a longer playthrough are fine.

REPORTED: policy wording quoted by Metro and TheGamer (Nov 4 2026); we couldn't load Rockstar's support page ourselves today

With reviews out and New Zealand already playing, the story is now in players' hands. That makes this the week spoilers spread fastest, and the week creators need to know where Rockstar draws the line.

What Rockstar says it can remove

The policy isn't new and doesn't name GTA 6. Metro reports it was last updated in January 2025 and covers every Rockstar game. These are the lines that matter for GTA 6:

  • Endings: "Posting video showing the ending of the game" is listed as spoiler content.
  • Cutscene edits: edited combinations of cutscenes or in-game plot dialogue, and other big story reveals.
  • Isolated cinematics: "isolated videos of the game's cinematics will be removed".
  • In-game TV and comedy: isolated in-game TV shows or comedy performances.
  • Pre-release footage: leaked footage and early unboxing videos, reportedly "regardless of how the game was obtained".
  • Cheating, mods and ports: footage that encourages cheating (including exploits and glitches) or promotes unauthorised mods and ports.
  • Any time: "Take-Two Interactive reserves the right to have its copyrighted material taken down at any time."
REPORTED: quotes as published by TheGamer and tbreak (citing Metro)

What is allowed

Normal gameplay is fine. The key line is that "it's ok to show cutscenes as part of a larger play-through". So a full walkthrough, a narrated Let's Play or a live stream that reaches a story scene is treated differently from a clip that is only the cutscene.

Not sure about a video? Rockstar's policy reportedly points creators to copyright@take2games.com. Check the official page before relying on any summary, including this one.

Will Rockstar actually enforce it?

Take-Two has a strong takedown record around GTA 6: it sent takedowns on leaked clips in August 2026, which Rockstar then confirmed were real (GameSpot, Engadget), and it struck a fan-made recreation of the GTA 6 map inside GTA 5. On the other hand, outlets note that GTA 5 ending videos uploaded days after its 2013 launch are still on YouTube.

OFFICIAL: Rockstar confirmed the August 2026 leaked clips were genuine and that takedowns were sentTHEORY: our guess is that standalone ending and cutscene uploads get hit first in launch week, while long playthroughs are left alone, matching how the policy is written. Rockstar hasn't said how it will enforce it for GTA 6

How to avoid GTA 6 spoilers this week

  • Mute words like "GTA 6 ending", "Jason", "Lucia" and "spoilers" on X and in YouTube comments for a few days.
  • Avoid thumbnails that say "ending", "final mission" or "plot twist".
  • Our story explainer only covers what Rockstar showed before launch, and our missions guide has no story beats.
  • Check when GTA 6 unlocks in your region so you can start before the spoilers find you.

Frequently asked questions

Will Rockstar take down GTA 6 spoiler videos?

It can. Rockstar's copyright policy lists ending videos, edited cutscene compilations and isolated cinematics as material Take-Two can remove at any time, and it applies after launch too.

Can I upload a GTA 6 playthrough with cutscenes?

Yes, per the reported wording: 'it's ok to show cutscenes as part of a larger play-through'. Full walkthroughs and narrated Let's Plays are treated differently from cutscene-only clips.

Is this a new rule just for GTA 6?

No. It's Rockstar's existing policy for all its games, reportedly last updated in January 2025. It doesn't mention GTA 6 by name.

Can I post GTA 6 glitch or money exploit videos?

The policy lists footage that encourages cheating, including exploits and glitches, as removable, so those videos carry a takedown risk.
