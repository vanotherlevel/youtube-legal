Onimusha Way of the Sword: Beat Every Boss, Best Weapons & Enemies

By vAnother Level · Updated 2026-11-12

This guide covers every boss in Onimusha: Way of the Sword in story order, with the attacks that kill people and the exact answer to each, plus a ranked list of the Oni Armaments, where to pick them up, and how to handle every common Genma. The short version: learn which attacks to deflect and which to dodge, absorb your souls before the enemy does, and spend every stamina break on a Break Issen.

Written for the launch version (released September 4, 2026). Boss order checked against several full walkthroughs.
Five rules that win most fights: (1) White-glowing attacks can only be parried or deflected, so never try to dodge-cancel them late. (2) Glowing-hand grabs (blue or purple tells) cannot be blocked: dodge them right before the hand connects. (3) Empty a boss's stamina with deflects, then pick a Break Issen mark: red for big damage, purple for a pile of souls. (4) Absorb souls the moment you have space, because some bosses (Daidara first) will eat them and power up. (5) The Oni Bow is not a gimmick: many bosses have a balloon, cord, neck or charge-up that you are meant to shoot.

How combat works (the short version)

Way of the Sword is a fast character-action game, not a Souls-like. Musashi has a light attack, a heavy attack, a dodge, a guard, and two timed defences built on the guard. You also carry the Oni Gauntlet, which pulls souls out of dead Genma, and the Oni Bow, which you can fire at will without spending Oni Power.

Issen (critical counter) timing

An Issen is a counter-attack you start a split second before the enemy's blow connects, which makes the window stricter than a parry but the reward far bigger. Several guides describe the same rhythm: the enemy's weapon flashes white with a metallic clang at the top of the wind-up, the flash fades, and you press attack as the forward swing begins. On big monsters there is often no weapon to watch, so listen for the rushing-air sound of the attack and press when it is loudest.

  • Start on swordsmen. Human-sized Genma with blades give the clearest white flash. Practise there before you try it on bosses.
  • Not everything can be Issened. Charges, grabs and many unarmed or elemental attacks are better dodged. PowerPyx notes, for example, that Byakue's charges are the one thing in his kit you should not Issen.
  • Chain Issen is an early skill (reported at two Oni Stones). After an Issen, your blade flashes white: press attack again before the full-screen white flash to jump to the next enemy. The window gets tighter with every link.
  • Break Issen opens when you empty an enemy's stamina. On bosses you choose where to strike: red marks deal the most damage, purple marks deal roughly half but drop far more souls. Against armoured bosses like Benkei the red marks are the armour plates you need to strip.
  • Critical Issen is reported to trigger off a perfectly timed deflect on a heavy swing, for much higher damage.
  • Oni Awakening (also called Full Awakening) is reported to make the Issen window very lenient. That is why it is the best time to chain counters on a boss.
  • Story mode widens all timing windows if you just want to see the game.

Soul absorption: what each colour does

Dead Genma release coloured souls that float on the ground for a short time. Nothing is collected automatically: you hold the Oni Gauntlet's absorb input to pull them in, and you can move while you do it, but you are open to hits until the pull ends. The rule is simple: make space first, then absorb.

  • Absorb mid-fight, not just after. Souls fade if you leave them, and several bosses compete for them.
  • Some enemies steal souls. Daidara eats loose souls to speed up his enhanced state, the Kogai parasite goes into a frenzy when it absorbs souls, and Dohatsu-ten grows stronger from absorbed souls.
  • Purple or pink unblockable attacks on some bosses drain your souls on hit and push the boss into a stronger phase. Treat that colour as "get out of the way".
  • Oni Awakening lasts until its gauge empties. While transformed, damage you take drains the gauge instead of your health, and you cannot use Oni Armaments. Hold your attack to pour the gauge into damage.

Oni weapons and elements

Unlike the original trilogy, your katana is fixed: the sword skins you unlock are cosmetic only and change no stats. Your real weapon choice is the Oni Armament you equip, a special attack fired from the blue Oni Power gauge. The guides we checked found no enemy elemental weakness chart in this game, so do not expect the old "lightning beats this Genma" rule. Elements live inside the armaments instead:

  • Fire: Wind-Whipper's whirlwind turns into a burning cyclone if you trigger it next to a torch or open flame. The Firebird Flute's birds set targets on fire and stagger them.
  • Lightning: The Flashing Void is a lightning spear, a slow-motion charged strike that hits from outside melee range.
  • Stamina break: The Earth-Shakers hammers are not elemental, but a single hit is reported to drain about half a boss's stamina bar and they crack armour.
  • Fire resistance matters for Shuten Doji. His whole moveset is fire, so bring fire-resistance items to that fight.
  • Do not waste the gauge on trash. The Oni Power gauge only refills with blue souls, so firing an armament at a weak enemy leaves you empty when the elite shows up.

Sasaki Ganryu

Where: Kiyomizu-dera Temple in Mission 2. This is the duel from the demo and the game's first real skill check.

  • Quick piercing thrusts: his fastest move. Deflect them; the final hit of a string is the safest one to parry.
  • Delayed wave-like strikes: he holds the swing longer than you expect. Wait for the actual forward motion instead of guarding early and bleeding stamina.
  • Leaping stomp: big and readable. Dodge it, or Issen it as he comes down for a free chunk of health.
  • Blue left-hand grab: unblockable. Dodge right before the hand reaches you.
  • Approach: everything he does is telegraphed. Stay close, deflect strings to drain his stamina, and take a Break Issen every time it breaks. Use the fight to learn the Issen rhythm on a sword user.

Daidara, Slavering Glutton

Where: the Oni Refuge, the floating-island area you reach straight after the Sasaki duel in Mission 2. He returns in the final mission's gauntlet.

  • The real threat is soul theft. Daidara eats souls lying on the floor, which speeds him into his enhanced state (his belly glows red). Pull souls in as soon as they drop.
  • Phase one: slow, heavily telegraphed mace swings and a running charge. Dodge the swings with focus and punish the recovery.
  • Mace lick: when he licks the mace and then swings, the hit throws you across the arena for heavy damage. Get out of range or dodge late.
  • Phase two: adds a blue hand grab. Dodge it with good timing.
  • Approach: short strings, then back off. He is one of the easier bosses as long as you keep his soul snack away from him.

Rasho-gan, Wish Warper

Where: Yasui Konpiragu Shrine in Mission 5. Beating him earns you a new Oni ability.

  • The cord: in phase one he is tethered by a cord you cannot see normally. Activate Oni Vision (hold L2, press R2 on PS5) to highlight it, then shoot it with the Oni Bow.
  • Malice orb swirls: deflect or parry them while holding guard.
  • Large bubble orb: shoot it down with the bow before it reaches you.
  • Leap and spin attacks: dodge sideways; parrying is not worth much against his ranged tendrils and eye beams.
  • Grab into gut pummel: dodge with precise timing or take a long, painful combo.
  • Phase two (enhanced): he starts uprooting and throwing trees. Keep your distance or perfect-dodge each throw, then close in during the recovery.

Byakue, Hundred Defilements

Where: Kiyomizu-zaka Slope in Mission 6. An enhanced version shows up again late in the game. A talisman-covered axe giant that grows stronger as he is bloodied.

  • Rule of thumb: dodge when he is moving, deflect when he is swinging.
  • Axe swings: deflectable and good Issen targets.
  • Shoulder charges and sprints: do not Issen these. Dodge to the side.
  • Blue talisman-hair lash: dodge right before the hair hits you.
  • Enhanced version: below half health he coats the axe in Malice. Jump just before the axe slam lands to clear the shockwave, and stay out of the Malice pools he leaves on the ground.

Ifuu, Fearsome Gale

Where: Kennin-ji Temple in Mission 9. The wind brother; he can fly after absorbing souls.

  • Close-range swings: easy deflects. Build his stamina damage here.
  • Leaping blue grab: dodge it.
  • Wind slashes: parry or deflect.
  • Planted-weapon thrust: parry while pushing the left stick toward the weapon's angle.
  • Air-blow: cannot be countered. Accept it and reset.
  • Phase two (enhanced): adds a deflectable spinning charge and more shoulder charges. When he inflates the balloon on his back and pulls you in, shoot the balloon with the Oni Bow. If he launches himself into the air, shooting the balloon drops him for free damage.

Nue (Blue and Red)

Where: Nijo-jo Castle in Mission 11. Two chimeras at once, and they come back in the final mission.

  • Camera: leave lock-on off. Hit the closest Nue and keep the other in view.
  • Claws, lunges, head and tail smashes: perfect-dodge most of them rather than guarding.
  • Tail guard: when a Nue raises its tail to cover its face, use the guard-break input (Triangle + Circle on PS5).
  • Lightning orbs: from about half health their tails join in and fire electric orbs. Deflect them back.
  • Order: focus one down completely, then finish the second with the same pattern.

Greater Nue

Where: later in Mission 11. A bigger, mutated Nue with lightning and separate body parts you can target.

  • Opener: a huge white-glowing tail swipe. White glow means parry or deflect, not dodge. Claw strikes follow.
  • Leaping body slam: dodge or Issen.
  • Sprint into claw scratch and shoulder charges: dodge to the side.
  • Blue worm-mouth grab: dodge with good timing.
  • Tail guard: guard-break input again when he raises the tail.
  • Cutting off the tail: possible, but PowerPyx advises against it because you can still take damage during the animation.
  • Phase two: the tail swipe gets larger with lightning orbs around it. About halfway through, lightning starts falling from the sky; keep moving and only attack in short bursts.

Dohatsu-ten, Heaven's Bane

Where: Toribeno in Mission 12. A horned, winged Genma that feeds on blood and souls. An enhanced version is fought later.

  • Katana strikes: deflectable.
  • Face kicks: perfect-dodge or deflect.
  • White-glowing attacks: parry or deflect only. He shouts "No escape" right before one.
  • Ranged sword slashes: deflect them back at him. His cues are "Shatter", "Prepare" or "Perish".
  • Guarding: if your hits stop doing damage, he is blocking. Use the guard-break input (Triangle + Circle).
  • Enhanced state: rapid teleports that end in a deflectable strike, Malice-coated hits, and a dash slash if you stand far away. Stay mid-range and react to the voice lines.
  • Enhanced version (later): the same fight, but he can open with enhanced-state attacks.

Benkei

Where: Gojo-ohashi Bridge in Mission 13. The warrior monk, now a mindless Genma.

  • Armour first: you cannot damage him until his armour is gone. Empty his stamina, then choose the red Break Issen marks on his leg guards, shoulders and arms.
  • Polearm attacks: deflect most of them. His committed rush combos have long recovery, so punish after the last hit.
  • Jump slams: save your Issen for these.
  • Phase two: he sets his back gear on fire, adds a lunging polearm thrust, and sometimes pulls a second polearm for a white-glowing flurry (parry or deflect only).
  • Best tool: the Earth-Shakers, if you already have them from Mission 11. Their stamina damage and armour cracking are made for this fight.

Burai, Lightning Keeper

Where: Kennin-ji Temple in Mission 15. Ifuu's naginata-wielding brother.

  • Lightning orbs: sidestep them.
  • Exposed eye: when the eye is open, slash it to pull out blue souls.
  • Kick: dodge or Issen.
  • Lightning crosses: run to the side.
  • Raised right leg: stop attacking and back off; a punishing kick is coming.
  • Phase two, the killer: he leaps out of view, drops a lightning bolt on you, then ground-slams. Keep moving the moment he disappears and dodge clear before the slam.
  • Mace spin: around the halfway mark he spins and seeds beam orbs, then a slam sends out a lightning shockwave. Leave immediately.

Sasaki Ganryu (second fight)

Where: Yasaka Pagoda in Mission 16, To Live is to Suffer. Sasaki is now fused with his Oni, Gioh, into one body with a large snake-like attachment. Single health bar, very little downtime.

  • Rapid multi-hit strings: deflect these instead of parrying; deflects hold up better across long combos. Answer with one or two quick hits.
  • Delayed grab and jump attacks (from about half health): unblockable. Dodge the grab after its pause and dodge the jump on landing, then counter.
  • Break Issen choice: the head gives the most damage; the snake attachment gives more souls and healing when you are low.
  • Heal in the gaps, right after a deflected string, never in the middle of one. Chip damage from guarding adds up fast here.

Shuten Doji, Gourd Drainer

Where: Mount Oe in Mission 18. A former Oni whose drinking heals and strengthens him. Bring fire-resistance items.

  • Regeneration: he heals over time, so do not back off for long. Keep steady pressure.
  • Bottle slam, leaping pierce, kicks: dodge or Issen.
  • Fire phase: around halfway he lights the bottle and drinks it. Dodge the fire trails, and shoot him with the bow when he inhales for a fire gout.
  • Bottle pull and white-glowing greatsword strike: deflect.
  • Phase two, the severed head: his head fights alongside him. Its blue charge must be dodged. His four-hit combo (body slam, head slam, body slash, fire ground attack) must be deflected, parried or Issened. Shoot down the head's balloon.
  • Use Oni Awakening here. Under a third of his health he regenerates more often, so a full transformation burst is the cleanest way to finish him.

Ifuu and Burai

Where: Kennin-ji Temple in Mission 20, Storm Chaser. Both brothers at once, with every move from their solo fights.

  • Lock-on off so you can watch both.
  • Keep their health even. If both bars are close, breaking one brother's stamina damages both.
  • Shared lunge: when either shouts "Let's end this!" or "Together!", both lunge at once. Deflect it.
  • When one hits phase two, switch focus to the brother who has not. Shoot Ifuu's balloon whenever he leaps.
  • Combined storm (both in phase two): lightning, then gusts, then a ramming dive. Parry or deflect the dive at the exact moment of contact.

Sasaki Ganryu (Altered) and Gioh

Where: Arashiyama in Mission 21, In the Light of an Icy Moon. Two phases with separate health bars for Sasaki and Gioh. PowerPyx rates this the second-hardest fight in the game on Carnage.

  • Sasaki: faster katana strings, a slow leaping slash that is an easy deflect, and a fast white-glowing thrust that can one-shot you on Carnage. Deflect it, never dodge late.
  • On stamina break: take the purple souls and transform into an Oni.
  • Gioh: quick deflectable sword strikes plus tentacles you must track even while pressuring Sasaki. When she stretches her neck to drain the red flowers, shoot the neck with the Oni Bow. Dodge her blue mouth grab.
  • Phase two: when Sasaki shouts "Smash him flat, Gioh!" her purple tentacles add a slam and a neck lash, both dodgeable or Issen-able. About a third of the way in, Gioh coats his sword in Malice and the lethal thrust comes back. Dodge or deflect the Malice ground waves.

Dokyo, The Transgressor

Where: Hozukyo Gorge, Arashiyama, also in Mission 21. A ranged trickster; the fight is a chase, so close the gap and land quick hits.

  • Spiky balls: counter, dodge or outrun. The giant one can be deflected straight back.
  • Electrified barriers: weave through the gaps.
  • Golden circle with balloon: shoot the balloon.
  • Fire turrets in a line: stand between two of them.
  • Double with an electric net: use Oni Vision to find the glowing real Dokyo and shoot her.
  • Mask lock-in: get behind her so the exploding enemies hit her instead of you.
  • Phase two: constant orbs; dodging them fills a gauge for a special attack. Deflect the returning spiky ball twice and shoot the red mask. A long pause means a blink-in staff swing is coming: deflect or guard.
  • Planet attack (one-shot on Carnage): find the single unbroken rock and get behind it immediately, then throw a shard at her.

Benkei (Fully Armed)

Where: Rendaino in Mission 22, The Clash at Rendaino. Benkei returns with daggers, axes and a cannon, and opens with his old phase-two moves. Save before this mission (point of no return).

  • On stamina break: strip armour, or take yellow, blue or purple souls depending on what you need.
  • Cannon throw: dodge the cannon, then jump when fire cracks appear. The detonation takes close to half your health.
  • Daggers: deflect them back. Spinning axes: dodge.
  • Blue grab: dodge sideways (press dodge while pushing the stick to the side) just as it comes.
  • Hammer charge: shoot it with the Oni Bow immediately. If it fires, it is an instant kill on Carnage.
  • Fireballs: create distance.

Shanaoh Castle rematch gauntlet

The final mission, The Way of the Sword, sends you up through Shanaoh Castle and makes you beat earlier bosses again before the last fight. Every source lists Daidara, the Nue pair and Greater Nue; some walkthroughs also list Byakue and Dohatsu-ten here (the Boss Rematch menu has "Enhanced" versions of both). Guides report the exact order differently, so we will confirm it and update this section. Use the strategies above; the main change is that you now have a full kit, so lead with Earth-Shakers or Still Water on every stamina break.

Minamoto no Yoshitsune (final boss)

Where: the top of Shanaoh Castle in Mission 23. Three phases, and the hardest fight in the game by most accounts. PowerPyx suggests carrying two of each: Amulet of Introspection, Stimulant, Greater Defense Talisman, Greater Might Talisman and Heavy Restorative, plus one Sun-Dried Hozuki (capped at one).

  • Phase one: his double-leg kick can be deflected or parried, but a perfect dodge or Issen knocks him down for free hits. Three huge white-glowing slashes: dodge at range, deflect up close. His delayed grab must be avoided: if it lands, he drains your souls and powers up. When he taps his shoulder, parry the lightning-fast thrust that follows.
  • Phase two: opens with an airborne slam that cracks the ground; sprint away as soon as you can move. He is far more mobile, so rely on deflects over Issen. Normal attacks barely touch his stamina and armour breaks are unreliable. Expect arrows from both sides, a three-hit white-glowing combo, and a long-range warp grab. When he shouts "Shatter" or cracks the ground, sidestep or perfect-dodge. Take purple souls on every stamina break.
  • Phase three: he only takes damage while you are in Oni Awakening. Build the gauge by chaining Issen counters (blue and purple souls) or by parrying into Blazing and attacking. When he pulls souls toward himself, grab as many as you can, then dodge or Issen his arena-wide slash. Once your gauge is full, transform, Issen as much as possible and hold attack until the gauge is almost empty.
  • After the credits: reload your save and visit the well behind Rokudo-chinnoji Temple to collect his Genma Note.

Upgrade tips

  • Upgrade the sword track first. It raises regular attack, Issen power and Oni Armament power together.
  • Oni Stones are limited. One playthrough gives you 54, and fully upgrading all six gauge armaments needs 33 of them, so you cannot max everything. Pick two armaments and commit. Unspent stones carry into New Game Plus.
  • Reported full-upgrade costs: Earth-Shakers 5 Oni Stones and 5,500 red souls; Still Water 6 Oni Stones and 6,500 red souls.
  • Buy Chain Issen early. It is cheap and turns every group fight into a soul farm.
  • Oni Vision treasure skills (Treasures, Treasures 2, Search Range) make the hidden armament chests much easier to spot.
  • Early-game build: Two Celestials for sustain until Mission 4, Wind-Whipper for crowds from Mission 4, then switch to Earth-Shakers in Mission 11 and keep them for the rest of the game, with Still Water as your second pick once you reach Arashiyama.

Frequently asked questions

How many bosses are in Onimusha: Way of the Sword?

There are 16 main boss encounters in the story (counting Sasaki Ganryu three times, Benkei twice, and Ifuu and Burai together once), plus the rematch gauntlet in the final mission. The post-game Boss Rematch menu lists 18 fights, including enhanced versions of Byakue, Dohatsu-ten and Benkei.

Who is the final boss of Onimusha: Way of the Sword?

Minamoto no Yoshitsune, fought at the top of Shanaoh Castle in Mission 23, The Way of the Sword. He has three phases, and in the last one he only takes damage while you are in Oni Awakening.

How do you do an Issen in Onimusha: Way of the Sword?

Press attack a split second before the enemy's strike lands. On sword users, watch for the white flash and clang at the top of the wind-up, let it fade, and attack as the swing comes forward. Chain Issen lets you jump to more enemies if you press again before the full-screen white flash.

What do the soul colours do?

Yellow souls heal you, blue souls fill the Oni Power gauge for Oni Armaments, red souls pay for upgrades, and purple souls build Oni Awakening. You have to absorb them manually with the Oni Gauntlet, and they fade if you leave them.

What is the best weapon in Onimusha: Way of the Sword?

Your katana is fixed and its skins are cosmetic, so the real choice is your Oni Armament. Earth-Shakers are the best overall because they wreck boss stamina and armour. Still Water is the best endgame pick for building Oni Awakening, and The Flashing Void is the safest single-target burst.

Can you miss Oni Armaments?

Yes. Most of them are in hidden chests, and the Earth-Shakers in the Underground Laboratory are the easiest to miss. Guides list The Clash at Rendaino (Mission 22) as the point of no return, so collect them before then.

Is Onimusha: Way of the Sword a Souls-like?

No. Capcom built it as a fast action game. It has stamina and parries, but there is a Story mode with wider timing windows, and the combat rewards aggressive counters like Issen rather than slow, cautious play.

Does Onimusha: Way of the Sword have elemental weaknesses?

Not in the way the original trilogy did. The guides we checked found no enemy weakness chart. Elements come from the armaments instead: Wind-Whipper and the Firebird Flute use fire and The Flashing Void uses lightning. Shuten Doji is the one boss where fire resistance really matters.
