Ocarina of Time Remake: How to Beat Every Boss, Best Weapons & Enemies

By vAnother Level · Updated 2026-11-12
Home › Guides › Zelda: Ocarina of Time Remake Guides

  1. Every boss (in order)
  2. Best weapons
  3. Normal enemies

The Ocarina of Time remake on Switch 2 keeps the same ten dungeon and final bosses as the 1998 game, and every one of them is still beaten with the item you found in its dungeon. This guide walks you through every boss and mini-boss in story order, ranks the best weapons and tells you how to get each one, then covers how to handle every common enemy.

Where the remake changed a fight, we say so and name where that comes from: our own play-throughs of the footage for our channel videos, the reviews, or other guides. Where we could not confirm a change, the strategy is the one from the original game, and it still works because Nintendo kept the core of every fight.

Five tips that make every boss easier: 1) The boss always wants the item from its own dungeon. If a fight feels impossible, you are probably not using it. 2) Lock on (Z-targeting, now the left trigger) and keep your shield up; in the remake, locking on also raises the shield. 3) Bring at least one bottle with a fairy or a potion. 4) Use the new manual jump and roll-dash to get out of the way, but remember that running off a ledge no longer auto-jumps. 5) Enemies no longer respawn when you leave a room (reported by previews), so arrows, seeds and bombs are harder to farm. Stock up in shops before a boss.

Queen Gohma (Inside the Deku Tree)

Needs: Fairy Slingshot (or Deku Nuts)

The first boss, the Parasitic Armored Arachnid, waits in the basement of the Deku Tree. Look up when you enter: she hangs from the ceiling. Her body is armoured, so the only place to hurt her is her big eye.

  • Stun her: when her eye glows red (open), hit it with a Fairy Slingshot seed. A Deku Nut flash also stuns her if you are close.
  • Punish: run in while she is flat on the floor and slash the eye with the Kokiri Sword. A Deku Stick does double damage if you have one.
  • Gohma Larvae: when she climbs back up she drops eggs that hatch into small spiders. Kill them fast with the sword; don't let them crowd you while you aim.
  • Her charge: when she runs at you, back off or roll to the side, then aim again once she stops.
  • Remake changes: the remake shows glowing triangle markers around her eye when she can be hit, which makes the window much clearer. One guide (games.gg) says she now covers her eye with her front legs while she is immune, so wait for the eye to open rather than spamming seeds.

Lizalfos (Dodongo's Cavern mini-boss)

Two lizard warriors who take turns attacking you in a room with lava around the edge. You meet them twice in the dungeon. Keep your shield up and lock on: they jump in with a slash, and you hit back as soon as they land or finish a swing. When one jumps away, the other takes over. Watch the lava gaps and don't strafe into them. The remake makes Lizalfos hit harder (FinalBoss review), so don't trade hits.

King Dodongo (Dodongo's Cavern)

Needs: Bombs or Bomb Flowers

The Infernal Dinosaur lives in a lava pit at the bottom of the cavern. You need the Bomb Bag from this dungeon (or the Bomb Flowers around the arena, which you can pick up with the Goron Bracelet).

  • The opening: when he stops and breathes in to spit fire, throw a lit bomb straight into his open mouth. Navi warns you when he starts to inhale.
  • Punish: he swallows the bomb, explodes and falls over. Run up and slash his head. A Deku Stick hits hard here.
  • The roll: after he gets up he curls into a ball and rolls around the arena. Run along the outer edge in the same direction or move out of his line. In the remake he leaves a spark trail, so his path is easy to read.
  • Fire breath: if you miss the bomb, step out of the fire's line, sideways, not back.
  • Remake: reports said he has new attacks; we could only confirm he brings in Baby Dodongos on a later recovery (games.gg). The bomb-in-mouth method is unchanged. He finally rolls into the lava when beaten.

Big Octo (Inside Jabu-Jabu's Belly mini-boss)

Remake: arena redesigned

A huge Octorok circling a watery room. In the original it moved around spinning spikes in the middle and kept reversing direction. In the remake the spikes are gone and it no longer turns around at random (RPG Site, FinalBoss, games.gg), which makes this fight much less annoying.

  • Hit its face (a Boomerang throw or a sword hit) so it turns and flees.
  • Chase it around the ring and slash the glowing green weak spot on its rump. Navi points this out.
  • Repeat until it collapses. With no random reversals, you can stay right behind it.

Barinade (Inside Jabu-Jabu's Belly)

Needs: Boomerang

The Bio-Electric Anemone hangs from the ceiling of Jabu-Jabu's stomach. Everything here is done with the Boomerang you found earlier in the belly. Never touch it with the sword while it is charged with electricity.

  • Phase 1: throw the Boomerang at the tentacles that hold it to the ceiling. Cut all of them while dodging its electric zaps along the floor and the lasers from its core.
  • Phase 2: a ring of Bari jellyfish spins around its body. Boomerang the core when it stops spinning to stun it, then slash the jellyfish, or hit the jellies directly when they break off.
  • Phase 3: with the jellies gone, it moves around the room. Boomerang its core to stun it, then run in and slash. Deku Sticks deal big damage while it is stunned.
  • Tip: keep moving in a circle; its zaps travel along the floor toward where you were standing.

Stalfos (Forest Temple mini-boss)

Remake: acid pool added

Skeleton swordsmen with shields. You fight one, then a pair that guards the Fairy Bow. They block a lot, so don't swing wildly. Hold your shield up, let them swing first, then hit back with a quick combo or a jump attack. A bomb dropped at their feet or the Megaton Hammer (on a later visit) breaks their guard.

Remake change: the second Stalfos room no longer has a hole in the middle. It is now a shallow acid pool that hurts you but not them, and more Stalfos rise from it (IGN via FinalBoss, respec.net, FinalBoss review). Stay on dry ground and pull them to you. The remake also makes Stalfos hit harder.

Poe Sisters and Meg (Forest Temple mini-bosses)

Meg's fight: reported changes

Joelle (red), Beth (blue) and Amy (green) are fought in their wings of the temple after you solve their painting puzzles. Shoot each one with the Fairy Bow while she is visible, then lock on and slash. Each returns her flame to a torch in the main hall. In the remake each wing is colour-coded to its Poe, and the green Poe's sliding-block painting puzzle no longer has a time limit (IGN via FinalBoss).

Meg (purple) is the last sister. She splits into copies that circle you, and only the real one can be hurt. In the original, the real Meg was the one that spun once before the copies appeared. Previews of the remake report that her fight now has three rounds with a different tell each round (her flames or her eye expression), and that the Hookshot can damage Poes (respec.net, IGN). Look closely at each copy before you shoot; a wrong guess makes them all attack you.

Phantom Ganon (Forest Temple)

Needs: Fairy Bow, Master Sword

The Evil Spirit from Beyond rides his horse through the paintings around the arena. This is the classic 'tennis' fight.

  • Phase 1 (paintings): he and a decoy ride toward the front of two paintings. The real one rides all the way out of the frame; the fake turns back. Shoot the real one with the Fairy Bow as he comes out. Three hits end the phase. Dodge his lightning when he flies over the arena.
  • Phase 2 (tennis): he floats in the air and throws a ball of energy. Swing the Master Sword at it to send it back. Keep the rally going until it hits him, then he falls to the floor. Run in and slash.
  • His lightning on the ground: when he sends lightning along the floor instead, roll sideways.
  • Remake changes: the fight is easier. In the original he drifted after each shot and sped up every volley. In the remake his speed stays slow, the ball always goes back when you hit it, and only the final, clearly signalled volley speeds up (RPG Site; also games.gg). games.gg reports a new dash attack in his last phase, so be ready to roll.

Flare Dancer (Fire Temple mini-boss)

A dancing column of flame that guards the route to the Megaton Hammer. You can't hit it while it burns. Wait until it stops spinning and stands in place, then hit it with the Hookshot (or slash it) to knock the flames off. Its small core jumps out and runs around the room. Chase it and slash it before it climbs back onto its pedestal and relights. Repeat three or four times. Wear the Goron Tunic and keep away from the flame trail it leaves as it dances.

Volvagia (Fire Temple)

Needs: Megaton Hammer, Goron Tunic

The Subterranean Lava Dragon lives in a stone arena dotted with lava holes. You need the Goron Tunic to survive the heat and the Megaton Hammer from this temple.

  • The head pop: Volvagia surfaces from one of the holes and looks around. Run to that hole and smash its head with the Megaton Hammer before it dives again.
  • Punish: when its head lies stunned on the floor, switch to quick sword slashes (or the Biggoron's Sword for big damage).
  • Flying phase: after a while it flies up and breathes fire across the arena or drops burning rocks from the ceiling. Watch the shadows on the floor and keep moving; roll out from under falling rocks.
  • Tip: don't stand on the edge of a hole. Stand between holes so you can reach whichever one it uses.
  • Remake: reports mention new attack patterns, which we could not confirm. The hammer method is unchanged.

White Wolfos (Ice Cavern mini-boss)

An icy wolf that guards the Iron Boots at the end of the Ice Cavern. Like all Wolfos it blocks attacks from the front and backflips away. Hold your shield up, let it lunge, then hit it right after the claw swipe, or circle behind it and slash its back. Don't throw sword hits at its face. You get the Iron Boots, and Sheik teaches you the Serenade of Water afterwards.

Dark Link (Water Temple mini-boss)

Remake: fog added, sword-stand trick kept

You find him in the misty room with shallow water and a dead tree. He copies your moves, so the usual Z-targeted sword fight goes nowhere: he blocks what you block and swings when you swing. The remake keeps his famous trick of jumping onto your blade, adds more fog, and gives him a sharper design.

  • Best answers: the Megaton Hammer (he can't copy it well), Din's Fire (hits him all around you), or the Biggoron's Sword for big hits.
  • Jump attacks: a jump slash while he is coming at you often lands.
  • Don't over-target: stop locking on now and then and swing at him from an angle; locking on makes him easier to mirror.
  • Grab the Longshot from the chest in the next room. It is the key to Morpha.

Morpha (Water Temple)

Needs: Longshot (Hookshot works)

The Giant Aquatic Amoeba is a floating nucleus that controls the water in its arena. The water itself can't be hurt; only the small red nucleus can.

  • Pull it out: when the nucleus rises inside a water tentacle, fire the Longshot at it. It is pulled out onto the floor.
  • Punish: slash it while it flops around on dry ground. It wriggles back into the water quickly, so hit it fast.
  • Avoid: being grabbed by a tentacle; it throws you against the spikes on the wall. Stay away from the edges and away from the middle, and step back from tentacles that rise right next to you.
  • Later in the fight: it raises several tentacles at once. Only one holds the nucleus. Shoot that one.
  • Corner tip (original): in the N64 game, standing in a corner made it hard for the tentacles to reach you. Try it if you get thrown a lot.

Dead Hand (Bottom of the Well and Shadow Temple)

You meet this one twice: first as young Link in the Bottom of the Well, where it guards the Lens of Truth (now 'Lenses of Truth', a pair of goggles in the remake), then as adult Link in the Shadow Temple, where it guards the Hover Boots route. Grey hands stick out of the floor. If one grabs you, wiggle the stick and press buttons to break free. Dead Hand itself walks out and leans in to bite you. Hit its head when it bends down, then back off before it bites. Five or so sword hits end it. You can also slash the hands to make it come closer. It is slow; never stand still next to it.

Bongo Bongo (Shadow Temple)

Needs: Fairy Bow or Hookshot, Lens of Truth helps

The Phantom Shadow Beast is a giant pair of hands that beats a drum you stand on. His body is invisible without the Lens of Truth.

  • Stun both hands: shoot each hand with the Fairy Bow or the Hookshot. When both are stunned, his eye opens.
  • Hit the eye: shoot the eye with an arrow, then run up and slash it while he is down.
  • The drumming: each slam bounces you up. Wearing the Hover Boots keeps you from bouncing on the drum, which makes aiming much easier.
  • Watch for: the hands slapping together on you and pounding down. Keep away from them and stay near the edge of the drum.
  • The Lens: with it you can see him charging at you; it drains magic, so use it in bursts.

Gerudo Thieves (Gerudo's Fortress mini-bosses)

To rescue the four carpenters you fight a sword-swinging Gerudo Thief in front of each cell. They block often, flip backwards and swing two scimitars. Keep your shield up, let them finish an attack, then punish with a quick combo or a jump slash. Each one drops a small key. Between fights, sneak past the guards; if a guard catches you, you are thrown back in the cell. Arrows and the Hookshot stun guards for a few seconds. Your reward is the Gerudo's Membership Card, which opens the Haunted Wasteland gate and the Gerudo Training Ground.

Iron Knuckles and Nabooru (Spirit Temple mini-bosses)

Huge armoured knights with axes. Young Link fights one before reaching the Silver Gauntlets, and adult Link fights more later, the last of them being Nabooru, brainwashed by Twinrova. Iron Knuckles walk slowly but each axe swing hurts badly and breaks pillars.

  • Never block their axe; get out of the way. Let them swing, then step in and hit them while the axe is stuck in the floor or on the follow-through.
  • Hit them from the side or behind. As they take damage their armour falls off and they get faster.
  • As young Link: bombs and Deku Sticks deal more than the Kokiri Sword. The remake footage shows a young Link beating it with bombs.
  • As adult Link: the Megaton Hammer and Biggoron's Sword end these fights quickly.
  • Use the pillars to block their swings at range, but be ready: the axe breaks them.

Twinrova (Spirit Temple)

Needs: Mirror Shield

The Sorceress Sisters, Koume (fire) and Kotake (ice), fly on brooms over a ring of four platforms. Everything is done with the Mirror Shield from this temple. Reviews say the fight was eased in the remake, like Phantom Ganon, but we have no confirmed details beyond that.

  • Phase 1: raise the Mirror Shield toward the beam. Reflect Kotake's ice beam into Koume, and Koume's fire beam into Kotake. Each sister is only hurt by the other's element.
  • Phase 2: they merge into Twinrova. She fires single blasts of fire or ice. Absorb three blasts of the same element with the shield, then fire the charged beam back at her. If a blast of the other element hits the shield, your charge is wasted.
  • Punish: when she falls, run to her platform and slash.
  • Stay mobile: move between the platforms so a beam never catches you with your shield facing the wrong way.
  • Nayru's Love protects you for a short time if you are about to die.

Ganon's Tower mini-bosses (Dinolfos, Stalfos, Iron Knuckles)

Before Ganondorf, the tower makes you clear three rooms of mini-bosses in the original layout: a pair of Dinolfos, a pair of Stalfos and a pair of Iron Knuckles. Use the methods above. Dinolfos are like Lizalfos but also breathe fire, so block and step to the side. The Megaton Hammer and Biggoron's Sword save a lot of time. Stock up on hearts and magic at the pots between rooms.

Ganondorf (Ganon's Tower)

Needs: Master Sword, Light Arrows

The Great King of Evil fights you from the air above a floor of breakable tiles.

  • Position first: his opening attack breaks part of the floor around the middle. Stay near the outer edge.
  • Tennis again: swing at his energy ball to send it back, exactly like Phantom Ganon. When it hits him, he is stunned.
  • Light Arrows: a Light Arrow also stuns him directly. Fire one while he is charging an attack.
  • Punish: when he drops down stunned, jump to his platform and slash.
  • Multi-orb attack: when he throws a spread of small orbs, a charged spin attack sends them back.
  • Supplies: if you run low, drop down to the lower level and break pots for magic and arrows.
  • After the fight: you escape the collapsing tower with Zelda. One guide (games.gg) says the escape no longer has a timer in the remake. You face two Stalfos at a fire barrier and ReDeads near the end.

Ganon (Ruins of Ganon's Castle)

Needs: Biggoron's Sword or Megaton Hammer, Light Arrows, Master Sword

The final boss. Ganon is bigger in the remake and, in his beast form, rains blood-red lightning and charges on all fours (seen in remake footage; Dexerto). The core of the fight is the same as in the original.

  • The Master Sword is knocked away at the start. Equip the Biggoron's Sword (best) or the Megaton Hammer. You can switch in the menu mid-fight.
  • His weak spot is his tail. Roll under him when he steps forward, or bait a swing of his twin blades and run around his side to slash the tail.
  • Light Arrows: a Light Arrow to his face stuns him, giving you time to run behind him.
  • Get away after each hit: he turns and swings his blades back at you.
  • Grab the Master Sword back when Zelda tells you to, then keep hitting his tail. Zelda holds him with her power, and the final blow must be with the Master Sword.
  • Supplies: breaking the rubble around the arena can drop hearts, arrows and magic.
  • Remake reports: some guides describe extra late phases, such as glowing weak points and a weak point on his forehead that Zelda exposes for Light Arrows (games.gg). Other guides describe the tail-only method. We could not confirm the exact order, so expect a few new moves after you recover the Master Sword.

Optional: Big Poes (Hyrule Field)

Ten large ghosts appear in Hyrule Field for adult Link while you ride Epona. This is how it works in the original; we did not confirm the remake's spots. Ride near their spots, shoot one with arrows from horseback until it falls, then catch its soul in an empty bottle. Sell them to the Poe Collector in the Ghost Shop. All ten win you a bottle.

Remake Great Fairy changes: every Great Fairy now asks for a rupee offering. In the footage we checked: Din's Fire + magic meter (Death Mountain, 25), improved spin attack that fires a magic wave (Hyrule Castle, 50), Farore's Wind (Zora's Fountain, 100), doubled magic (Death Mountain Crater, 200), Nayru's Love (Desert Colossus, 300) and halved damage (outside Ganon's Castle, behind a Golden Gauntlets pillar, 500). The halved damage gift makes every late boss much easier.

Early-game picks: as young Link, the Kokiri Sword plus Deku Sticks for bosses and the Slingshot for anything out of reach. Pick up the Hylian Shield from the Kakariko graveyard (pull back the grave at night) early; it doesn't burn like the Deku Shield. As soon as you're an adult, the Hookshot from Dampé's race makes the Forest Temple much easier, and the Megaton Hammer is the best general weapon until you finish the Biggoron trade.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Ocarina of Time remake have new bosses?

No. It has the same ten bosses as the N64 game, from Queen Gohma to Ganon, and the same mini-bosses. Some fights were changed: Big Octo lost its spikes, Phantom Ganon and Twinrova were made easier, and Ganon has new moves. The new Hall of Fallen Foes lets you rematch bosses.

What is the hardest boss in the Ocarina of Time remake?

Most players point to Bongo Bongo or Ganon, and the mini-boss Dark Link. Bongo Bongo is easier with the Hover Boots on, Dark Link with the Megaton Hammer or Din's Fire, and Ganon with the Biggoron's Sword and the halved damage gift.

How do you beat Dark Link in the Ocarina of Time remake?

He mirrors your sword moves. Use the Megaton Hammer, Din's Fire or the Biggoron's Sword, use jump slashes, and stop Z-targeting him now and then. The remake keeps his trick of standing on your blade.

What is the best sword in Ocarina of Time?

The Biggoron's Sword. It does twice the Master Sword's damage and never breaks, but it is two-handed. You get it at the end of the adult trading quest from Biggoron on the Death Mountain summit.

Is Master Quest in the Ocarina of Time remake?

Not playable. The game launched without it. Dataminers found unfinished Master Quest code that appears to be tied to DLC, but Nintendo has not announced it.

What is the Hall of Fallen Foes?

A new remake area with statues of the monsters you have beaten. You can read about each one or take on timed boss rematches and boss rushes, with your best time saved and rewards like decorations for Link's house.

Where do you get Din's Fire in the Ocarina of Time remake?

From the Great Fairy of Magic on Death Mountain as young Link. Play Zelda's Lullaby at her fountain and offer 25 rupees; she also gives you the magic meter.

How do you beat Ganon in the Ocarina of Time remake?

When the Master Sword is knocked away, use the Biggoron's Sword or Megaton Hammer and hit his tail by rolling under him or running around his side. Stun him with Light Arrows. Recover the Master Sword when Zelda calls out and use it for the final blow.
